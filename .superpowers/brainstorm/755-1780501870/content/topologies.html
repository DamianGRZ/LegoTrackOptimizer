Three ways to pack track densely into the 500×500 box

Key physical fact: the only curve is R40 = 40-stud radius. A 180° U-turn (8×R40) is therefore 80 studs wide. That single number makes or breaks each shape. Click the one you want to pursue.

A · Nested loops → one spiral circuit ★ recommended

~5 concentric racetracks, 8–11 studs apart. The corners are R40, the straights pack tight. A crossing (or switch) joins ring to ring into a single circuit. Densest: uses all 80 R40 (16/ring) + most straights → ~85–95%. Crossings earn their keep as ring connectors.

B · Boustrophedon serpentine — the trap

Looks densest, isn't. The R40 U-turns force rows 80 studs apart, so only ~6 rows fit in 500 → ~70–76%, and it still needs a long return path to close. Intuitive but the fixed radius fights you.

C · Double spiral (in-and-out)

Closes by construction without any crossing. Dense (~80–90%), but fixed-radius R40 can't make a smooth spiral — it collapses into "nested loops" anyway, just harder to parameterize. Effectively a clumsier version of A that ignores the crossing pieces.

My recommendation: A. It's the densest, the corners use exactly the R40 inventory, and it's the natural home for CROSS_90/DOUBLE_CROSSOVER — which raises F[0] directly. B is the cautionary "obvious but sparse" option; C achieves density but wastes the crossings.
