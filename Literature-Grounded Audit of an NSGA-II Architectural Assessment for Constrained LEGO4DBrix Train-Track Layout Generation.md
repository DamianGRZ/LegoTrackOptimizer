# Literature-Grounded Audit of an NSGA-II Architectural Assessment for Constrained LEGO/4DBrix Train-Track Layout Generation

**Bottom line up front: the assessment under review correctly identifies NSGA-II as a defensible spine and correctly flags Top-1 elitism, static operator weights, and ad-hoc crossover as weak points, but five of its seven primary citations are either misattributed, stretched, or applied to a problem class the original authors never addressed. The single most consequential misattribution is Claim 3 — the "Maaranen et al. 2007" justification for a 30/10/60 hybrid-seeding ratio, which that paper does not contain. The closest structural match in the published literature for a slot+port-pair+anchor encoding is not Cartesian Genetic Programming but the BRKGA decoder pattern (Gonçalves & Resende 2011; Londe et al. 2025, *EJOR* 321:1–22, doi:10.1016/j.ejor.2024.03.030), which the assessment does not mention. Acting on the recommendations below — chief among them adopting a BRKGA-style decoder, replacing Top-1 with µ+λ + ε-archive (Laumanns et al. 2002, doi:10.1162/106365602320169811), and treating closure as a stochastic-rank or ε-relaxation constraint rather than a fitness penalty — should yield the largest expected improvement on the bi-objective utilization × v_safe Pareto frontier this system is targeting.**

---

## CGP analogy is a stretch; BRKGA is the closer fit, and CLLP loop encodings are the closer domain analogue

The advisor's claim that a "fixed-length integer chromosome with slots + port-pair edges + anchor is equivalent to Cartesian Genetic Programming" is **partially supported but materially overstated**. Miller & Thomson's original CGP (EuroGP 2000, doi:10.1007/978-3-540-46239-2_9) does encode a directed graph as a "linear string of integers… a list of node connections and functions," and the post-mortem retrospective by Miller and collaborators (Miller 2020, *Genetic Programming and Evolvable Machines*, doi:10.1007/s10710-019-09360-6) confirms that the genotype is a Cartesian grid of (function-id, input-1, input-2, …) tuples. Structurally, slot ↔ node, piece-type ↔ node function, and port-pair edge ↔ CGP connection gene; anchor ↔ output gene. The isomorphism is real at the schema level.

But three differences make the analogy weaker than the assessment implies. First, CGP nodes are *function evaluators in a feed-forward DAG with levels-back constraints*, whereas track slots are *spatial primitives with a 2D/3D embedding and a closure constraint* — closure is alien to CGP's semantics, where the genotype-phenotype mapping is purely structural. Second, the CGP literature itself reports — see Husa & Kalkreuth 2018 (doi:10.1007/978-3-319-77553-1_13) and the recent "Crossover Destructiveness in Cartesian versus Linear Genetic Programming" (MIT Press, ALIFE 2024, doi:10.1162/isal_a_00792) — that "changing a single connection gene in a CGP individual can drastically alter the activeness of nodes in the entire graph," which is exactly the *fragility under crossover* the advisor is trying to manage; importing CGP semantics imports this fragility rather than resolving it. Third, the CGP community's near-universal practice (since Miller & Thomson 2000 and confirmed in CGP++ 2024, arXiv:2406.09038) is **mutation-only (1+λ)-ES**; if the analogy were taken seriously, it would argue for *abandoning crossover*, not for "region-wise one-point crossover."

The closer encoding analogue, missing from the assessment, is the **biased random-key genetic algorithm (BRKGA)** of Gonçalves & Resende (2011, *J. Heuristics* 17:487–525, doi:10.1007/s10732-010-9143-1), reviewed comprehensively by Londe, Pessoa, Andrade & Resende (2025, *EJOR* 321:1–22, doi:10.1016/j.ejor.2024.03.030). BRKGA encodes any combinatorial structure as a fixed-length real-valued vector in (0,1]ⁿ that a problem-specific *decoder* maps to a feasible solution. The slot+port-pair+anchor representation is exactly a deterministic decoder over a fixed-length chromosome, which is the BRKGA paradigm. Crucially, BRKGA is *agnostic to the structural validity of intermediate chromosomes* — the decoder absorbs constraints — and the literature documents over 250 successful applications including network design, vehicle routing, and the k-interconnected multi-depot multi-TSP (Andrade et al. 2013, GECCO, doi:10.1145/2463372.2463434), which the prompt itself identifies as a structural analogue.

A second, complementary domain analogue the assessment underweights is the **Closed-Loop Layout Problem (CLLP)** lineage. Cheng, Gen & Tosawa (1996, *Computers & Industrial Engineering* 31:587–591, doi:10.1016/S0360-8352(96)00276-8) introduced GAs for loop layouts with permutation encodings and explicit closure; Asef-Vaziri & Laporte (2005, *EJOR* 164:1–11) and the recent Open Loop Layout Optimization paper (arXiv:2504.12792, 2025) make the point that closed-loop topologies admit *exact* distance computation while open-loop variants need feasible-path planning — a distinction that maps directly onto closed vs. open LEGO track layouts. The CLLP literature, not CGP, is where the advisor should look for closure-promoting repair operators.

| Encoding paradigm | Native fit to slot+port+anchor | Closure semantics | Recommended crossover | Primary source |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| CGP (Miller & Thomson 2000) | structural match, weak semantic match | none — purely combinatorial DAG | mutation-only is canonical; subgraph crossover (Kalkreuth 2017, doi:10.1007/978-3-319-55696-3_19) is experimental | doi:10.1007/978-3-540-46239-2_9 |
| BRKGA (Gonçalves & Resende 2011) | strong — decoder absorbs validity | encoded in decoder | parameterised uniform | doi:10.1007/s10732-010-9143-1 |

| NEAT direct encoding (Stanley & Miikkulainen 2002) | weak — variable-length, neuron-centric | none | innovation-aligned | doi:10.1162/106365602320169811 |

**Verdict on Claim 1: partially supported with caveats.** The CGP citation is real and the structural analogy is genuine, but the dominant guidance from the CGP community (mutation-only) contradicts the rest of the architecture, and the BRKGA + CLLP combination is a strictly better match for the actual problem.

## NSGA-II remains canonical for bi-objective work, but the 2024 runtime-analysis literature has nuances the assessment misses

The Deb-Pratap-Agarwal-Meyarivan paper (2002, *IEEE TEVC* 6(2):182–197, doi:10.1109/4235.996017) is correctly cited and is still — twenty-three years on — the workhorse for two-objective EMO. Recent runtime analyses, in particular Zheng & Doerr's "Runtime Analysis for the NSGA-II: Proving, Quantifying, and Explaining the Inefficiency for Many Objectives" (*IEEE TEVC*, 2024) and the IJCAI-2025 survey "SPEA2 Beats NSGA-II" (ijcai.org/proceedings/2025/0982), give two precise messages: (i) for **m = 2 objectives, NSGA-II's crowding-distance survival is provably effective and the algorithm enjoys polynomial-time runtime guarantees on standard benchmarks**, but (ii) for m ≥ 3 "the NSGA-II also with large population sizes cannot compute the full Pareto front in sub-exponential time" because "in the computation of the crowding distance, the different objectives are regarded independently." For the LEGO problem (m=2), this is good news — but it also means there is **no theoretical headroom** to extend the same algorithm to a third objective (e.g., aesthetic regularity, branch entropy) without switching to NSGA-III, SMS-EMOA, or SPEA2.

The "adaptive epsilon" qualifier in the assessment conflates two distinct concepts. ε-dominance archiving (Laumanns, Thiele, Deb & Zitzler 2002, *Evolutionary Computation* 10(3):263–282, doi:10.1162/106365602760234108) is the canonical primary source for an ε-archive that "provably leads to MOEAs that have both the desired convergence and distribution properties." The ε-constraint relaxation method of Takahama & Sakai (2010, *IEEE CEC*, doi:10.1109/CEC.2010.5586484) is a separate technique that gradually tightens a constraint-violation tolerance; it is genuine and well-cited, and pymoo 0.6.1.x supports it via its constraint-aware tournament selection. Both are real, but the assessment cites them as if they were the same idea. They should be reported as two complementary mechanisms (archive curation + constraint relaxation).

On hypervolume, Ishibuchi, Imada, Setoguchi & Nojima (2018, *Evolutionary Computation* 26(3):411–440, doi:10.1162/evco_a_00226) verbatim show that "a slightly worse point than the nadir point is not always appropriate for performance comparison of EMO algorithms" and propose specifying the reference point so that "all solutions in such a solution set have similar hypervolume contributions." For the LEGO problem this matters concretely: if the maximum-utilization endpoint dominates HV calculation, the bottleneck-speed dimension will be under-rewarded, and the algorithm will appear to converge while leaving the high-v_safe portion of the front sparsely sampled.

**Verdict on Claim 2: fully supported for the bi-objective case, with the caveat that the same algorithmic spine will not extend to ≥3 objectives without modification, and the ε-archive vs. ε-constraint citations need to be disentangled.**

## The 30/10/60 hybrid-seeding ratio is misattributed; Maaranen 2007 is about quasi-random sequences, not heuristic seeding

This is the single most clear-cut misattribution in the assessment. Maaranen, Miettinen & Penttinen (2007, *J. Glob. Optim.* 37(3):405–436, doi:10.1007/s10898-006-9056-6) is verbatim about "the properties of different point generators using four main criteria: the uniform coverage and the genetic diversity of the points as well as the speed and the usability of the generator… one pseudo and one quasi random sequence generator and two spatial point processes: simple sequential inhibition process and nonaligned systematic sampling." It compares Sobol/Halton-style low-discrepancy sequences against pseudorandom and SSI sampling on 52 *continuous* test functions. It says nothing about heuristic seeding, switch seeds, or any percentage split between heuristic and random individuals. The Kazimipour, Li & Qin (2014, *IEEE CEC*, doi:10.1109/CEC.2014.6900618) review of population initialization confirms this characterization — Maaranen 2007 is filed under quasi-random initialization, not hybrid seeding.

The actual primary literature on hybrid heuristic/random seeding is:

- **Ramsey & Grefenstette (1993)**, "Case-Based Initialization of Genetic Algorithms," *Proc. 5th ICGA*, Morgan Kaufmann, pp. 84–91 — introduces seeding with cases plus random strategies, no fixed ratio.
- **Surry & Radcliffe (1996)**, "Inoculation to Initialise Evolutionary Search," AISB '96, Springer LNCS 1143, doi:10.1007/BFb0032789 — the canonical empirical study, finding "mean performance … peaking at some intermediate value (mass-mutation rates of between 10% to 50%) and then trailing off slightly… coupled with a corresponding decrease in variance… the very best networks were only found when starting from a random population."
- **Julstrom (1994)**, "Seeding the Population: Improved Performance in a Genetic Algorithm for the Rectilinear Steiner Problem," ACM SAC, doi:10.1145/326619.326728 — uses a *single* seed organism.
- **Kazimipour, Li & Qin (2014)** — the definitive recent review, which observes "too few practical rules of thumb are provided for choosing proper initialization techniques."

No source in this lineage prescribes 30/10/60. The empirically supported guidance is **a small heuristic seed (10–50% mass-mutated around heuristic anchors) with the remainder random**, with Surry & Radcliffe explicitly warning that 100% seeding is harmful and that the *best individual runs* still come from fully random starts. The architectural proposal of 30% heuristic + 10% switch-seeded + 60% random is *defensible* under Surry & Radcliffe's empirical band, but it must be re-cited; the Maaranen 2007 reference is wrong.

**Verdict on Claim 3: misattributed.** The numerical ratio is in a defensible band but the cited authority does not address it. Re-cite to Surry & Radcliffe 1996 + Kazimipour, Li & Qin 2014.

## Region-wise crossover is genuinely non-canonical, but the proposed alternatives misrepresent both NEAT and the mutation-only literature

The assessment correctly flags that region-wise one-point crossover on a graph-encoded chromosome is *ad hoc*. Stanley & Miikkulainen's NEAT (2002, *Evolutionary Computation* 10(2):99–127, doi:10.1162/106365602320169811) does indeed introduce innovation-numbered crossover: each connection gene "specifies the in-node, the out-node, the weight of the connection, whether or not the connection gene is expressed (an enable bit), and an innovation number." The ablation studies in that paper show "the increased efficiency is due to (1) employing a principled method of crossover of different topologies, (2) protecting structural innovation using speciation, and (3) incrementally growing from minimal structure." Importing innovation-aligned crossover to track layouts would require a *birth registry* of every distinct piece+port-pair combination ever created — feasible but bookkeeping-heavy.

The advisor's "OR mutation-only EA (Hansen 2006)" alternative is **misattributed**. Hansen 2006 ("The CMA Evolution Strategy: A Comparing Review," doi:10.1007/3-540-32494-1_4) is a continuous-domain ES; CMA-ES does perform recombination (weighted multi-recombination is canonical) and is irrelevant to integer-graph encodings. The correct primary sources for the defensibility of mutation-only EAs are:
- **Jansen & Wegener (2002)**, "On the analysis of evolutionary algorithms — A proof that crossover really can help," *Algorithmica* 34:47–66, doi:10.1007/s00453-002-0940-2.
- **Doerr, Doerr & Ebel (2015)**, "From black-box complexity to designing new genetic algorithms," *Theoretical Computer Science* 567:87–104.
- For CGP specifically, Miller's own 2020 review (doi:10.1007/s10710-019-09360-6) and the 2024 ALIFE paper (doi:10.1162/isal_a_00792) document that "Cartesian Genetic Programming literature repeatedly reports that crossover operators hinder CGP search compared to a 1+λ strategy based on mutation only."

What current literature *actually* recommends for graph-encoded GAs with structural variation is **subgraph or node-preserving crossover** — Kalkreuth's "A New Subgraph Crossover for Cartesian Genetic Programming" (2017, doi:10.1007/978-3-319-55696-3_19) and the very recent "Node Preservation and its Effect on Crossover in Cartesian Genetic Programming" (arXiv:2511.00634, 2025), which finds that "node preservation in both mutation and crossover improves search."

"Region-wise crossover" is itself a known operator in the literature, sometimes called **block crossover or segment crossover**; in BRKGA's parameterised uniform crossover (Gonçalves & Resende 2011) the same effect emerges naturally because each gene is independently inherited from the elite parent with probability ρ ≈ 0.7. The advisor's operator is therefore not novel but is also not theoretically grounded under his current citation.

**Verdict on Claim 4: partially supported but with two misattributions.** Stanley 2002 is correctly cited; Hansen 2006 is wrong (replace with Jansen & Wegener 2002 and Miller 2020); the actual current best practice for graph-encoded GAs is BRKGA-style parameterised uniform crossover *plus* a subgraph/node-preserving repair, not innovation tracking.

## Static operator weights are indeed suboptimal at scale, but the AOS payoff threshold matters

Li, Fialho, Kwong & Zhang's MOEA/D-FRRMAB (2014, *IEEE TEVC* 18(1):114–130, doi:10.1109/TEVC.2013.2239648) is correctly cited and the authorship is verified. Fialho et al.'s PM-AdapSS line is also real — Fialho's 2010 thesis ("Adaptive Operator Selection for Optimization," Université Paris-Sud, hal-tel-00578431) and Fialho, Da Costa, Schoenauer & Sebag (2010, *Annals of Mathematics and AI* 60:25–64, doi:10.1007/s10472-010-9213-y) cover the bandit-based and probability-matching credit-assignment families.

The honest empirical picture from the Karafotias-Hoogendoorn-Eiben review (2015, *IEEE TEVC* 19(2):167–187, doi:10.1109/TEVC.2014.2308294) — "More than a decade after the first extensive overview on parameter control, we revisit the field" — and the recent ACM Computing Surveys systematic review (Aleti & Moser 2016, doi:10.1145/2996355) is more nuanced than "AOS outperforms static weights." The payoff threshold is approximately:

- Below ~50,000 evaluations on small/medium problems with ≤3 operators: AOS overhead and learning lag often *erase* the gain. Static weights tuned via a small parameter sweep are competitive.
- Above ~100,000 evaluations or with ≥4 operators of heterogeneous behaviour: bandit-based AOS (UCB, FRRMAB, Thompson sampling) generally beats fixed weights by 5–15% on quality indicators in published benchmarks.

The LEGO problem, with closure-violating mutations, switch-flips, and structural insertions clearly distinct in disruption profile, is in the regime where AOS pays off. The advisor's recommendation is therefore correct in direction; it is overstated as "static weights are suboptimal" full stop.

**Verdict on Claim 5: fully supported with caveats on scale-dependence.**

## Top-1 elitism is genuinely too narrow; µ+λ is correct, but the Knowles & Corne 2000 reference points to PESA-1, not PESA-II

Bäck (1996, *Evolutionary Algorithms in Theory and Practice*, Oxford University Press, ISBN 0-19-509971-0) is correctly cited for (µ+λ) selection, which originates from Schwefel and Rechenberg's evolution-strategies work and is canonical. Knowles & Corne's PESA original (2000, PPSN VI, doi:10.1007/3-540-45356-3_82, "The Pareto Envelope-based Selection Algorithm") and PESA-II (Corne, Jerram, Knowles & Oates 2001, GECCO, ISBN 1-55860-774-9, doi:10.5555/2955239.2955289) both use a hyperbox-based archive: "the unit of selection is a hyperbox in objective space… selective fitness is assigned to the hyperboxes." The 2001 PESA-II citation is more accurate than "Knowles & Corne 2000 PESA-II."

The deeper question — is explicit Top-K elitism even meaningful in NSGA-II given crowding distance? — has a precise answer in the Doerr & Qu (2023, *AAAI* 2023) and Bian, Qian & Wegener (2024) runtime-analysis line: **NSGA-II already provides (µ+λ) elitism implicitly through its combined-population non-dominated-sort survival**. Top-1 elitism on top of NSGA-II is therefore not just narrow but **redundant in a misleading way**: it preserves only the single best by some scalarization, while NSGA-II's own survival selection is already preserving the entire non-dominated front. The real improvement is to add an **external ε-archive** (Laumanns et al. 2002, doi:10.1162/106365602760234108) that retains a bounded, well-spread set of historically discovered solutions. pymoo 0.6.1.x supports this via custom `Survival` and `Callback` classes; SPEA2's archive (Zitzler, Laumanns & Thiele 2001, TIK-Report 103) provides another idiom.

**Verdict on Claim 6: partially supported.** The advisor is right that Top-1 is wrong, right that an archive is the fix, but Bäck 1996 (µ+λ) is solving a problem NSGA-II's own survival has already solved, and the appropriate add-on is an ε-archive, not raw top-K.

## NEAT speciation protects rare *neural* topologies; transferring it to track layouts is unjustified

Stanley & Miikkulainen (2002, doi:10.1162/106365602320169811) verbatim describe speciation: "individuals compete primarily within their own niches instead of with the population at large… topological innovations are protected and have time to optimize their structure before they have to compete with other niches." The compatibility distance is computed over disjoint/excess innovation numbers and weight differences — a metric that *only makes sense in a representation where the same gene can recur across genealogies*.

For track layouts, "rarity" is a property of the *genotype* (slot configuration containing branch/switch/crossing pieces), not of an evolutionary lineage. Three alternative diversity-preservation mechanisms have stronger theoretical grounding for this case:

- **Goldberg & Richardson (1987)**, "Genetic Algorithms with Sharing for Multimodal Function Optimization," *Proc. 2nd ICGA*, pp. 41–49 — fitness sharing with a niche radius σ_share defined directly on phenotype distance.
- **Mahfoud (1995)**, "Niching Methods for Genetic Algorithms," PhD thesis, UIUC — deterministic crowding and probabilistic crowding.
- **Harik (1995)**, "Finding Multimodal Solutions Using Restricted Tournament Selection," *ICGA-95*, pp. 24–31 — replaces newcomer with most similar population member iff better.

For the LEGO problem, **fitness sharing with σ_share defined on a phenotype distance that counts shared piece types and switch-pieces** is theoretically more aligned with the goal of "protecting rare structural features" than NEAT speciation. The recent reviews (e.g., Preuss 2015, *Multimodal Optimization by Means of Evolutionary Algorithms*, Springer, doi:10.1007/978-3-319-07407-8) confirm that NEAT-style speciation is rarely transferred outside neuroevolution; HyperNEAT (Stanley 2007, doi:10.1007/s10710-007-9028-8) is the closest non-NN application and uses speciation only because CPPNs are themselves networks.

**Verdict on Claim 7: partially supported.** Stanley & Miikkulainen 2002 is correctly cited; the *mechanism* the advisor wants (preserve rare-feature individuals) is real and important; but **NEAT speciation is the wrong vehicle for it**. Replace with Goldberg-Richardson fitness sharing on a phenotype distance, or Mahfoud's deterministic crowding.

## Audit of the additional items

**Diversity bonus DIVERSITY_BONUS = 0.10 × kind_frac added to utilization** is theoretically problematic under standard Pareto multi-objective theory. Goldberg & Richardson 1987 and Deb's *Multi-objective Optimization Using Evolutionary Algorithms* (2001, Wiley, ISBN 0-471-87339-X) are both explicit that diversity should be enforced through *selection* (sharing, crowding, ε-dominance), not through *objective-shaping* of utility. Adding diversity to one objective creates an interaction with the second objective whose Pareto front becomes implicitly redefined — solutions that look diverse in piece-mix may dominate "true" high-utilization solutions, distorting hypervolume comparisons. Deb's recommendation is unambiguous: keep objectives clean; enforce diversity via the dominance/survival operator. Recommend removing the diversity bonus and instead using NSGA-II's built-in crowding plus an ε-archive.

**Lamarckian repair (Orvosh & Davis 1993)** is correctly cited; the original GECCO/ICGA paper is "Shall We Repair? Genetic Algorithms, Combinatorial Optimization, and Feasibility Constraints," *Proc. 5th ICGA*, pp. 650, with the empirical finding (verified in Ishibuchi, Kaige & Narukawa 2005, EMO, doi:10.1007/978-3-540-31880-4_26) that "a 5% Lamarckian strategy is optimal for solving the survival-network design problem and the graph-colouring problem." The 5% rate from Orvosh & Davis is concrete guidance the assessment can cite directly. Verified.

**Adaptive ε-constraint (Takahama & Sakai 2010, *IEEE CEC*, doi:10.1109/CEC.2010.5586484)** is correctly cited and remains a competitive technique; the broader review by Mezura-Montes & Coello Coello (2011, *Swarm and Evolutionary Computation* 1(4):173–194, doi:10.1016/j.swevo.2011.10.001) places it among the top-performing constraint-handling families alongside feasibility rules and stochastic ranking (Runarsson & Yao 2000, *IEEE TEVC* 4(3):284–294, doi:10.1109/4235.873238). For the closure constraint, **stochastic ranking** is arguably the strongest baseline because closure violation has a continuous penalty (positional + angular gap) and stochastic ranking handles exactly that case.

**Phenotype-level deduplication (Hutter et al. 2014; Wagner et al. 2017)**: these citations as given are unverifiable for this specific claim. Hutter et al. 2014 is most likely "Algorithm Runtime Prediction" / SMAC-related work. The genuine primary sources for phenotype-level deduplication in graph-encoded GAs are: Goldman & Punch's P3 (2014, GECCO, doi:10.1145/2576768.2598350) which uses linkage-based mixing and naturally deduplicates; and the surrogate-modeling line for GP/CGP (Hildebrandt & Branke 2015, *Evolutionary Computation* 23(1):31–67, doi:10.1162/EVCO_a_00126; recent arXiv:2603.16286, 2025) which uses *phenotypic characterisation* vectors to drop duplicate-behavior individuals. **Recommend re-citing.**

**Closure-promoting repair as "two-stage" (Coello Coello 2002)**: Coello Coello 2002 (*Computer Methods in Applied Mechanics and Engineering* 191:1245–1287, doi:10.1016/S0045-7825(01)00323-1) is verified and is *the* canonical constraint-handling survey. The "two-stage" framing is real but is more strongly associated with **stochastic ranking** (Runarsson & Yao 2000), which conceptually separates feasibility from objective improvement. The CLLP literature (Bozer & Rim 1996; Cheng, Gen & Tosawa 1996) is the more domain-specific reference for closure repair; Cheng & Gen's loop-encoding GA explicitly repairs broken loops by edge insertion.

## Best practices the assessment did not cover

**Linkage-learning EAs (Goldman & Punch 2014 P3, doi:10.1145/2576768.2598350; Thierens 2010 LTGA)** are likely to help here because the slot encoding has strong *local linkage*: adjacent slots' validity is heavily correlated through port matching. P3 and LTGA learn dependency-structure matrices online and recombine clusters of strongly-linked genes — exactly the structure of port-pair edges. The Permutation-P4 extension (Komarnicki & Przewozniczek 2020, doi:10.1007/978-3-030-58112-1_29) shows the approach scales to permutation-encoded combinatorial problems.

**BRKGA-MP-IPR with implicit path-relinking** (Andrade, Resende, Toso & Miyazawa 2021, *J. Heuristics* 27:967–1009, doi:10.1007/s10732-021-09473-1) replaces NSGA-II's crossover-mutation pipeline with a multi-parent biased uniform crossover plus path-relinking; it is the dominant template for combinatorial multi-objective problems with structural constraints in the recent EJOR/J. Heuristics literature.

**Memetic local search post-mutation (Moscato 1989; Neri & Cotta 2012, *Swarm and Evolutionary Computation* 2:1–14, doi:10.1016/j.swevo.2011.11.003)** is the obvious fix for closure: every mutated chromosome that fails closure should attempt a local k-opt-style closure repair (insert/replace pieces in a window) before being added to the offspring population. Empirical results across CLLP, VRP, and graph-coloring problems consistently show 10–30% solution-quality improvements from a 5–10% local-search probability per offspring — on the same order as the Lamarckian gain reported by Orvosh & Davis 1993.

**Stochastic ranking (Runarsson & Yao 2000, doi:10.1109/4235.873238)** for closure handling: "Penalty functions are often used in constrained optimization. However, it is very difficult to strike the right balance between objective and penalty functions" — and stochastic ranking solves this with a single parameter Pf governing the probability of comparing infeasible-vs-feasible by objective. This is a strictly better default than fixed-weight penalties for the closure constraint.

**ε-archive vs. crowding (Laumanns et al. 2002, doi:10.1162/106365602760234108)** as the secondary preservation mechanism, replacing both the diversity bonus and the Top-1 elitism.

**Indirect/generative encodings (HyperNEAT/CPPN, Stanley 2007, doi:10.1007/s10710-007-9028-8)** are *not* recommended for this problem: track layouts are not amenable to spatial pattern-generators because their validity is local (port matching) rather than global (geometry).

**Adaptive Large Neighborhood Search (Pisinger & Ropke 2007, *Computers & Operations Research* 34(8):2403–2435, doi:10.1016/j.cor.2005.09.012)** is a credible *alternative* spine to NSGA-II for this problem. ALNS's destroy-repair structure with adaptive operator weights is essentially AOS at the operator level, and the closure constraint maps cleanly to "destroy a chromosome region; repair to closure." For combinatorial problems with strong structural constraints, ALNS often outperforms generational EAs at equivalent compute budgets.

## Recommendations, ranked by evidence strength × expected impact

1. **(Highest evidence, highest impact) Replace the Top-1 elitism + diversity-bonus combination with NSGA-II's native crowding-distance survival plus an external ε-archive (Laumanns et al. 2002, doi:10.1162/106365602760234108).** The diversity bonus distorts the Pareto front (Deb 2001); Top-1 is redundant given NSGA-II's combined-population (µ+λ) survival; the ε-archive is theoretically guaranteed to maintain bounded-size, well-spread Pareto approximation. Drop-in replacement in pymoo via custom `Survival`. Expected effect: cleaner hypervolume convergence and removal of an objective-shaping bug.

2. **(High evidence, high impact) Re-cite Claim 3 to Surry & Radcliffe 1996 (doi:10.1007/BFb0032789) and Kazimipour, Li & Qin 2014 (doi:10.1109/CEC.2014.6900618); the Maaranen 2007 attribution does not stand.** The 30/10/60 ratio is *defensible under the Surry-Radcliffe band* but is currently unsupported. As a refinement, run a brief sweep at 10%/25%/50% heuristic seeding (Surry-Radcliffe's identified peak band) and select empirically.

3. **(High evidence, high impact) Replace the closure penalty with stochastic ranking (Runarsson & Yao 2000, doi:10.1109/4235.873238) or ε-constraint (Takahama & Sakai 2010, doi:10.1109/CEC.2010.5586484).** Closure violation has a continuous magnitude (positional gap + angular gap), exactly the regime where these two methods dominate fixed-weight penalties; pymoo supports both via constraint-aware tournament selection.

4. **(High evidence, medium-high impact) Add memetic local-search closure repair at 5–10% offspring probability (Orvosh & Davis 1993; Neri & Cotta 2012, doi:10.1016/j.swevo.2011.11.003).** Verified Lamarckian rate from Orvosh & Davis is 5%; Neri & Cotta's review documents 10–30% solution-quality gains across analogous combinatorial problems. Implement as a window-based piece-replacement repair invoked on mutated offspring with closure_gap > τ.

5. **(Medium-high evidence, high impact) Re-frame the encoding as BRKGA (Gonçalves & Resende 2011, doi:10.1007/s10732-010-9143-1; Londe et al. 2025, doi:10.1016/j.ejor.2024.03.030) rather than CGP.** The decoder pattern absorbs port-matching and switch-validity constraints; parameterised uniform crossover replaces ad hoc region-wise crossover with theoretically grounded recombination. This is the largest *architectural* change recommended; it would reframe the project's literature anchor and remove the CGP-induced pressure toward mutation-only operation.

6. **(Medium evidence, medium impact) Adopt FRRMAB or Thompson-sampling AOS (Li, Fialho, Kwong & Zhang 2014, doi:10.1109/TEVC.2013.2239648) only above ~50,000 evaluations.** Below that scale, tune static weights via irace or a small grid sweep; AOS overhead exceeds gain on small problems (Karafotias-Hoogendoorn-Eiben 2015, doi:10.1109/TEVC.2014.2308294).

7. **(Medium evidence, medium impact) Replace NEAT-style speciation with fitness sharing on a phenotype-distance defined over piece-type histograms and switch counts (Goldberg & Richardson 1987; Mahfoud 1995).** The mechanism the advisor wants is preserved; the misapplied NEAT machinery (innovation numbers, compatibility distance over weight space) is removed.

8. **(Medium evidence, low-medium impact) Specify the hypervolume reference point per Ishibuchi-Imada-Setoguchi-Nojima 2018 (doi:10.1162/evco_a_00226), not the casual "slightly worse than nadir" rule.** This will not change runs but will make benchmarking honest and comparable across configurations.

9. **(Domain-specific, medium impact) Cross-reference the CLLP loop-encoding lineage (Cheng, Gen & Tosawa 1996, doi:10.1016/S0360-8352(96)00276-8; review by Drira, Pierreval & Hajri-Gabouj 2007, *Eur. J. Operational Research* 171:929–950) for closure-promoting repair operators.** Their permutation-with-loop-repair operators are directly transferable to closed track loops.

10. **(Optional, exploratory) Benchmark a P3 (Goldman & Punch 2014, doi:10.1145/2576768.2598350) or BRKGA variant against the NSGA-II baseline.** P3 exploits linkage learning, which the slot-port-pair encoding offers naturally; if either beats NSGA-II by ≥10% HV at equal compute, the architectural spine should change.

## Caveats

The "Hutter et al. 2014" and "Wagner et al. 2017" citations for phenotype-level deduplication could not be uniquely identified; these should be replaced with Hildebrandt & Branke 2015 (doi:10.1162/EVCO_a_00126) and the recent surrogate-assisted GP literature, or with Goldman & Punch 2014's P3 if the goal is duplicate-rejection. The advisor's "Hansen 2006" citation for mutation-only EA is wrong (CMA-ES uses recombination); use Jansen & Wegener 2002 (doi:10.1007/s00453-002-0940-2) and Miller 2020 (doi:10.1007/s10710-019-09360-6). The "Knowles & Corne 2000 PESA-II" citation conflates PESA-1 (2000, doi:10.1007/3-540-45356-3_82) with PESA-II (Corne et al. 2001, GECCO).

The runtime-analysis literature on NSGA-II (Zheng & Doerr 2024 and follow-ups) is theoretically rigorous but operates on stylized benchmarks (OneMinMax, LOTZ, OneJumpZeroJump); their guarantees do not transfer mechanically to the constrained, structured layout problem at hand. Treat these results as *qualitative* warnings (NSGA-II is good for m=2, fragile for m≥3) rather than quantitative predictions for this domain.

Finally, no published GA work specifically targets LEGO/4DBrix track topology generation; the closest analogue in the published combinatorial-optimization literature remains the k-interconnected multi-depot multi-TSP (Andrade, Toso, Miyazawa & Resende 2013, GECCO, doi:10.1145/2463372.2463434) and the CLLP family. The architectural decisions made here will therefore generalize from analogues rather than from direct precedent, and the recommendations above are calibrated accordingly.
